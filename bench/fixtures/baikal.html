Lake Baikal: Facts and Figures

Lake Baikal is a rift lake in southern Siberia, Russia, between Irkutsk Oblast to the northwest and the Republic of Buryatia to the southeast. It is the oldest lake on Earth, estimated at 25 to 30 million years old.

Size and depth

Baikal is the deepest lake in the world. Its maximum depth is 1,642 metres, measured in the central basin. Its average depth is about 744 metres. The lake is 636 kilometres long and up to 79 kilometres wide.

By volume it is the largest freshwater lake in the world, holding about 23,600 cubic kilometres of water, roughly 22 to 23 percent of the fresh surface water on the planet.

Wildlife

More than 1,700 species of plants and animals live in and around the lake, about two thirds of which are found nowhere else. The best known is the Baikal seal, or nerpa, one of the few freshwater seal species.

Ice

The lake usually freezes from January to May. The ice can reach a thickness of up to two metres and is clear enough in places to see several metres down.
